11: ## 1. What an RRM Does
12: 
13: A **Review Rework Module** converts adjudicated review findings into bounded engineering work and independent verification. Its scope comes from approved dispositions, not raw report counts. It can repair completed frontend, backend, cross-cutting or release work. It does not consume the identity of the next BIM, FIX or FEAT and it does not change a product's phase numbering.
14: 
15: Success means: the accepted repair objectives are verified, required existing behavior remains green, unresolved items have explicit dispositions, and the certified QA branch is properly closed out and merged. No RRM automatically proves an entire phase or application production-ready.
16: 
17: **Module identity** follows the Factory-level rule (`SOFTWARE_FACTORY_PLAYBOOK.md` › Module Identity & QA Handoff): `RRM-<NNN>-<APP-SLUG>`, uppercase canonical ID, lowercase-kebab branch. Mechanics shared with every module type — one folder, one manager file, Plan Mode, verify-first, Engineer read-only Git inspection, Director-only Git mutations, and existing context-specific cloud restrictions, baseline-first regression, per-module failure policy, Definition of Done — are documented in `BIM_PLAYBOOK.md` §4–§12 and apply by reference. **Every RRM includes a module-specific `QAM/`** (`QAM_PLAYBOOK.md`; Director ruling RUL-01 and RUL-05).
18: 
19: ## 2. When an RRM Is Launched — the Two Review Checkpoints
20: 
21: The Factory has two planned independent-review checkpoints (Director ruling RUL-05, 2026-10-14):
22: 
23: 1. **Review after FFM build and its independent QA finish**; disposition findings and authorize RRM only when approved findings require repair.
24: 2. **Engine 2 review after all Engine 1 implementation phases and their QA are closed**, followed by approved RRM work. (Definitions and lifecycle vocabulary: `SOFTWARE_FACTORY_PLAYBOOK.md` §2, “Phase Vocabulary — One Canonical Map”.)
25: 
26: At each checkpoint: review findings require disposition (§5); approved findings determine the bounded RRM work; **a clean review produces no RRM** — repair work is never manufactured to fill a checkpoint. Review timing follows the approved project workflow; a review "both PASS" result defines neither Gate Q, Gate D nor release authority. Module QA certification (Gate Q), review completion and deployed-system verification (Gate D) remain distinct claims.
27: 
28: Revised workflow references: [FFM creation](_workflows/FFM_CREATION.md), [FFM execution and post-FFM review](_workflows/FFM_EXECUTION.md), [deployment and Gate D](_workflows/FFM_DEPLOYMENT.md), and [BIM through Engine 2](_workflows/BIM_WORKFLOW.md). Each includes a revised stick diagram and links its superseded original as history. The source diagrams are not current instructions.

30: ## 3. Inputs and Entry Conditions
31: 
32: The project Architect gathers:
33: 
34: - the original review reports, prompts, evidence and specimen identities;
35: - the current application state and project instructions/contracts;
36: - completed module records, known limitations and already-approved decisions;
37: - the intended use/release context, relevant environments and permitted actions;
38: - this playbook, the reviewer guidance (§11) and the governing QA doctrine (`QA_PLAYBOOK.md`, `QAM_PLAYBOOK.md`).
39: 
40: **Current-source recon is mandatory before writing the executable packet** (`RECON_QUESTIONNAIRE.md`). A report about old code cannot establish a defect in today's candidate by itself. For a review that was performed without Git access, compare the pertinent source bytes or excerpts with current source and record unresolved equivalence. Missing equivalence does not invalidate all findings, but it prevents pretending that reviewer disagreement is a controlled comparison.

42: ## 4. Lifecycle and Ownership
43: 
44: | Stage | Owner and action | Required exit evidence |
45: |---|---|---|
46: | Intake | Architect inventories reviews, specimens and limitations | Intake record and current-source reconciliation |
47: | Disposition | Architect proposes; Director rules | Every material finding has a reasoned disposition (§5) |
48: | Contract | Architect authors; Director approves; Architect instantiates `QAM/` from the Hub template set | Brief, frozen acceptance requirements, bounded engineering prompts, QAM skeleton |
49: | Plan / build | Engineer plans (Plan Mode), Director approves, Engineer implements and self-verifies | Execution log, engineering checks, factual `QAM/` handoff (`QAM_MANIFEST.md`, `HANDOFFS/ENGINEERING_HANDOFF.md`) |
50: | Candidate | Director commits/pushes the completed engineering work and creates `qa/<module-branch>` at that commit | Candidate SHA recorded by the QA Executor in Q1 |
51: | Q1 | QA Executor performs independent recon and drafts the test plan; QA Lead reviews and approves (with exact amendments if any); Director releases | Q1 review package; `QAM_APPROVAL.md` |
52: | Q2 | QA Executor executes the approved plan continuously on the QA branch | Evidence matrix, execution report, findings, result package |
53: | Repair loop (conditional) | QA Lead classifies; Architect/Director rule scope; Engineer repairs on the QA branch; Director commits; QA Executor re-pins and retests (Q4) | New candidate identity; bounded repair and regression evidence |
54: | Certification | QA Lead adjudicates and issues the Gate Q verdict | Acceptance and repair trace, limitations, verdict |
55: | Cleanup | QA Lead decides retention; authorized executor performs the required cleanup checkpoint (`QAM_PLAYBOOK.md` §14) | Cleanup report; durable records retained; debris handled |
56: | Closeout | Architect writes the closeout instruction; Engineer performs bounded closeout; Director reviews and commits | Closeout report, updated project status, clean tree |
57: | Merge / return | Director merges `qa/<module-branch>` into `main` and pushes; Architect returns lessons | Merge identity, journal inputs, deferred follow-ups |
58: 
59: The QA Lead and QA Executor enter at the QA handoff; they are not routine approvers of the Architect's engineering packet. One person may hold several seats; the responsibilities never merge (`QAM_PLAYBOOK.md` §3).

61: ## 5. Disposition Before Repair
62: 
63: Use one primary disposition for every material source finding:
64: 
65: - **ACCEPT REPAIR** — a current, applicable problem with an approved objective.
66: - **NEEDS DECISION / EVIDENCE** — an unresolved requirement, deployment fact or contradictory evidence; name the owner and the resolving question.
67: - **DEFER / FUTURE GATE** — retained with reason, owner and a concrete trigger; state whether current approved use is affected.
68: - **REJECT / NOT APPLICABLE / ALREADY RESOLVED** — with current evidence and reason.
69: - **DUPLICATE** — mapped to the retained finding while preserving original IDs and distinct evidence.
70: 
71: Record accepted risk only through a Director decision, with conditions and follow-up. Reviewer severity does not automatically approve a repair or a deferral. Both reviewers agreeing is not proof; one reviewer finding something alone is not a reason to dismiss it. Separate severity, origin and approved scope: an inherited issue may be severe and still require an explicit disposition.
72: 
73: Raw reports stay unchanged. Corrections and rulings append in the disposition/errata lane. The ledger must distinguish a source observation from a current-project decision. No raw review item silently becomes an authorized edit.
74: 
75: ## 6. Author the Engineering Contract
76: 
77: The Architect's packet contains a front door (the module manager, `BIM_PLAYBOOK.md` §5), the brief, the disposition ledger, the acceptance specification (`ACCEPTANCE_SPEC.md`, `SOFTWARE_FACTORY_PLAYBOOK.md` §2.5), the engineering prompts and the instantiated `QAM/`. Requirements are settled before the build. The Engineer delivers the acceptance artifact with the completion handoff but may not redefine success after implementation. Separate required behavior from engineering claims and from QA verdicts.
78: 
79: Each accepted objective maps to numbered ACs, preserved behavior, allowed surfaces, forbidden scope, verification level/environment and required evidence. **The contract is frozen at handoff; declared lanes inside the module (engineering handoff, recon, plans, evidence, findings, approved rulings) stay writable by their owners; contract changes require an attributable approved amendment in the append-only errata lane** (Director ruling RUL-04). No unresolved blocking decision enters implementation as an invented assumption.
80: 
81: Place the module under the project's established `agent_docs/` module convention; its QA records live in the module's `QAM/` structure (Director ruling RUL-03). Use staged prompts when dependency changes, schema work or distinct risks require checkpoints. Small related findings may share one RRM; split when independence, rollback or verification needs justify it.
82: 
83: ## 7. Engineering and Branch Flow
84: 
85: The Director starts from the approved current `main` baseline under project Git discipline. The Engineer performs Plan Mode inspection and reports conflicts before writes; the Director approves the plan and checkpoint progression; the Engineer implements only allowed work, verifies it, and logs changes, commands, outcomes, limitations and affected ACs. The Engineer may inspect Git read-only; the Director performs every Git mutation. Existing context-specific cloud restrictions remain in force; this document grants no new cloud permission.
86: 
87: **Branch flow (Director ruling RUL-02):** the module has a numbered engineering branch (for example `rrm-001-<app>`). After Engineering, the Director commits and pushes the completed engineering work and creates the matching `qa/<module-branch>` **at that same commit** — the completed engineering candidate. The Engineer performs authorized rework on that QA branch; the QA Executor independently tests on that same branch; role boundaries are preserved and conflicting simultaneous writes are avoided (one active writer when sessions share a worktree). The QA Lead certifies the governed candidate; the Architect issues the closeout instruction; the Engineer performs bounded closeout; the Director commits, merges the QA branch into `main` and pushes `main`. **Repairs are not transferred back to the engineering branch and the QA branch is not abandoned as the normal workflow.** Product, execution, evidence and closeout identities are preserved separately.
88: 
89: Review-only exports are separate input artifacts; their lack of `.git` does not change engineering's Git discipline. This is project-module policy; the Doctrine Hub's own synchronization Git rules are separate (`DEPLOYMENT_PLAYBOOK.md` §3).

91: ## 8. QA Handoff and Verification (the QAM interface)
92: 
93: Every RRM includes QAM: the Architect instantiates `QAM/` from the Hub template set at packaging; the Engineer completes the factual manifest and engineering handoff as part of the engineering assignment; the QA Executor performs Q1 (independent recon and test plan) and, after QA Lead approval and Director release, Q2 (continuous execution); the QA Lead adjudicates findings and certifies (`QAM_PLAYBOOK.md` §§5–14). The handoff supplies the approved spec and execution log plus references to the brief, the disposition ledger, the exact candidate commit, the repair diff, engineering results, fixtures/environment setup, limitations and the governing QA instructions (carried in `QAM/GOVERNING/` with version and hash provenance).
94: 
95: Tests must reach the boundary claimed: a direct function probe is not automatically a framework HTTP test; a synthetic database substitute is not deployed RLS evidence. Browser, SQL and deployment checks are required only where the approved contract or the QA risk plan needs them; report gaps instead of claiming success.
96: 
97: **Trace:** source finding ID → disposition → AC → repair → test/evidence → final disposition. Required regression establishes that protected existing behavior still works. Targeted re-review by the original reviewer can strengthen the evidence; the QA Lead specifies when it is required and who can satisfy it. A closed reviewer session does not invalidate the report or require resuming the same model instance.
98: 
99: The QA Executor records PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED, NOT RUN or ADJUDICATE with evidence; its recommendation is not the QA Lead's certificate. Verdict vocabulary is the Factory set in `QA_PLAYBOOK.md` §11; outstanding mandatory checks cannot be relabeled passed.
100: 
101: ## 9. Repair / Retest Loop (conditional)
102: 
103: QA Executor reports → QA Lead classifies → Architect/Director resolve scope or contract questions and issue a bounded repair instruction → Engineer executes the approved repair on the QA branch → Director commits → QA Executor re-pins and retests (Q4) → QA Lead re-adjudicates (`QAM_PLAYBOOK.md` §11).
104: 
105: A repair instruction names the affected findings/ACs, allowed files, prohibited changes and required engineering checks. Retest covers the diff, the impacted ACs and the required regression. Unchanged evidence may carry forward with explicit specimen provenance and impact reasoning. Do not restart the entire campaign without a concrete reason. New material issues require a disposition; no quiet scope expansion and no moving acceptance criteria to fit the implementation. Required final live validation runs against the final candidate after outstanding failures are resolved and the QA Lead authorizes it.
106: 
107: The RRM-001…004 campaign did not exercise a product repair round; this loop carries forward the governing doctrine and is not a capability proved by that pilot.
108: 
109: ## 10. Certification, Cleanup, Closeout and Merge
110: 
111: The QA Lead's Gate Q verdict certifies the implementation against the approved contract: it records the exact implementation SHA, accepted errata, results, preserved behavior, gaps and any Director-approved follow-ups. It does not certify deployment and it does not eliminate deferred findings.
112: 
113: **Sequence for every module with a `QAM/` (Director ruling RUL-03; `QAM_PLAYBOOK.md` §14):** accepted product verdict (QA Lead) → required cleanup under QA Lead authority, with records (credential and temporary auth-state hygiene; closing owned QA processes and browser contexts; authorized removal of disposable execution debris; retention of durable evidence, required attempts and useful helpers) → durable records committed (Director) → Architect closeout instruction → bounded Engineer closeout → Director merge of `qa/<module-branch>` into `main`. Product Gate Q, cleanup acceptance and Gate D remain separate claims. Verify that cleanup changed no product, test, configuration or contract bytes; a product change found at cleanup returns through QA, never buried in cleanup or closeout.
114: 
115: The closeout record distinguishes implementation, evidence, closeout and merge SHAs where they differ. Do not loop adding a commit's own hash to itself. After merge, the Director refreshes local `main` before any new branch starts. Required deployment and Gate D remain separate (`DEPLOYMENT_PLAYBOOK.md`; `QA_PLAYBOOK.md` §13).

117: ## 11. Reviewer Guidance
118: 
119: Reviewer guidance belongs here under RUL-14 (2026-10-16). The following proposed wording expresses that guidance for map review; pilot source records remain unchanged.
120: 
121: - **Freeze the specimen.** Name the exact revision, export or byte set reviewed; distinguish ordinary change review from broad or retrospective investigation.
122: - **Evidence levels.** Label every claim EVIDENCE / INFERENCE / CLAIM / GAP / QUESTION (`QA_PLAYBOOK.md` §8); state the conditions under which a finding holds and what would falsify it.
123: - **Origin ≠ severity.** Separate inherited origin from severity; disclose baseline failures without preventing security investigation; an inherited severe issue still needs a disposition.
124: - **No unsupported security claims.** A missing application-level filter is not a confirmed exposure when RLS or another effective boundary may apply; say what was and was not verified.
125: - **Calibrate.** State claim confidence and specimen equivalence; one severe, supported issue warrants correction — a repeated-incident threshold is not required.
126: - **Route, do not repair.** Reviewers propose; disposition is the Architect's proposal and the Director's ruling (§5). Original reports stay unchanged; dispositions and errata append.
127: 
128: ## 12. Continuous Execution Within a Released Assignment
129: 
130: Under RUL-15 (2026-10-16), execute continuously within a released assignment, including routine checks, reporting and packaging. Stop for concrete blockers, authority changes or required independent verdicts. This is not permission to cross a release boundary.
131: 
132: Prepare measurable scope, prerequisites, writable lanes and enumerated stops before release. Once released, include routine checks, reporting and packaging in continuous execution. Preserve required independent verdicts and authority boundaries. Report the full lifecycle effort, interruptions, repairs and missing-package follow-ups; never omit a valid stop to claim uninterrupted success.
133: 
134: ## 13. Return Learning
135: 
136: The project returns a concise retrospective: what was accepted, rejected or deferred and why; surprises in execution; the evidence needed to resolve disagreements; rework rounds and their causes; process friction; proposed doctrine changes. Attribute seat-authored lessons. Promote only supported lessons through the DocSet Sync process (`_SKILLS/factory-docs-update/`); a lesson is not doctrine until it lands in the Hub.
137: 
138: An RRM is complete only after actual delivery, QA and closeout evidence exists. Receipt of reviews and publication of a packet are preparation milestones, not a completed RRM.

12: ## 1. The rule every Architect must understand
13: 
14: **Every new Factory module MUST contain a complete, module-specific `QAM/` from initial authoring.** This applies to RRM, FFM, BIM, TRM, FIX, FEAT, ABM, APBM and future module types. The module's scope changes the tests; it does not remove QAM.
15: 
16: This rule governs modules newly authored after the Director's ruling of 2026-10-14. Modules already in flight on that date keep their recorded sequence until amended through their own ruling lane.
17: 
18: **Every ordinary QA engagement has TWO planned execution shots:**
19: 
20: 1. **Q1 — independent recon and test planning.** The QA Executor checks readiness, reads the contract, challenges the Engineer's claims and writes the proposed test plan. It returns one indexed review package and stops for the QA Lead.
21: 2. **Q2 — independent QA execution.** After the QA Lead approves that specific plan and the Director releases the candidate, the QA Executor executes the approved work continuously and returns one indexed result package.
22: 
23: **Repair and retest are conditional, after findings.** They are not mandatory extra shots. QA Lead adjudication, cleanup, certification, Architect closeout and Director Git actions still exist. Two-shot QA does not mean two messages from module creation through merge, and it never means ignoring a genuine stop condition.
24: 
25: The practical Director experience is: place one complete module pack, let Engineering finish its handoff, establish the QA branch, point the QA Executor to QAM, upload its Q1 ZIP, relay the QA Lead's approval, release Q2, and upload its result ZIP. The Director should not assemble QA documents, compose a new test campaign prompt, or perform repetitive browser matrices.
26: 
27: ## 2. What QAM is
28: 
29: QAM is the module's self-contained QA assignment, operating instructions, readiness checks, planning workspace and evidence record. It is a real folder inside the parent module, not merely a renamed empty QA folder or a reference to another chat.
30: 
31: The folder contains enough information for a fresh authorized agent to identify its role, determine the current phase, locate the governing contract, explain missing prerequisites and do the next permitted work. Module-relative links to the parent's canonical acceptance spec are acceptable; duplicate acceptance contracts are not required. External governing bodies must be available through a versioned local snapshot with provenance or an explicitly approved accessible source. In this Factory a snapshot of a Hub document is identified by its canonical name, its header version and the Hub commit SHA it was copied from; no doctrine release tag exists yet.
32: 
33: The Architect ships a prepared skeleton. The Engineer supplies factual completion inputs. The QA Executor independently drafts and executes tests. The QA Lead owns approval, adjudication and certification. A folder does not transport itself between applications: indexed ZIP export remains the predictable handoff when the QA Lead is in a separate chat.

35: ## 3. Authority and the five seats
36: 
37: | Seat | Responsibility | Boundary |
38: |---|---|---|
39: | Director | Approves scope/targets and risk decisions; controls credentials, Git, release and destructive authority | Does not have to perform every repeatable UI check |
40: | Architect | Defines frozen acceptance contract, module scope and protections; instantiates QAM from this playbook; resolves contract ambiguity; directs closeout | Does not substitute an engineering-green result for independent QA |
41: | Engineer | Implements approved scope, self-verifies, completes manifest and engineering handoff; repairs only ruled scope | Does not approve the QA plan, fill independent QA results or issue a certificate |
42: | QA Lead | Owns this playbook and standing risks; amends/approves Q1 plan; adjudicates findings; approves cleanup/retention and certifies | Does not invent missing evidence or silently repair the product |
43: | QA Executor | Performs Q1, drafts plan, executes approved Q2, records findings, executes approved retests and cleanup, exports packages | Does not self-approve, self-certify, mutate Git or make unauthorized product changes |
44: 
45: Names and models are interchangeable; authority follows the seat. The agent that engineered a candidate must not independently certify its own work. When one runtime changes seats, record the handoff and preserve an independent verification assignment and QA Lead review.
46: 
47: The Architect owns or validates acceptance criteria before implementation. The Engineer may propose clarifications during planning; approval and frozen-contract rules still apply. The Engineer's handoff reports what was built, not what the acceptance criteria ought to become after building it.

85: ## 6. Reliable entry through either runtime
86: 
87: Both `QAM/CLAUDE.md` and `QAM/AGENTS.md` must assign the QA Executor seat and refer to the same canonical `QAM_ENTRY.md`. Keep their routing text identical. At the parent module, both entry conventions must route Engineering to the implementation assignment and QA to QAM.

89: A user pointing an agent to QAM and asking it to start authorizes Q1 within the declared scope. A folder path by itself never authorizes Q2, product repair, certification, Git mutation or deployment. The agent reads persisted state before acting:

If a runtime does not auto-discover the entry, the documented fallback is one stable instruction to read `QAM_ENTRY.md`; there must be no long custom kickoff prompt containing missing operating rules.

110: 
111: Completing the factual QAM handoff is part of the original engineering assignment, not another trip to the Architect for a handoff-writing prompt. The Engineer records:
112:

114: - claimed behavior, self-verification commands/results and evidence links;
115: - install/build/serve/reproduction commands and working directory;
116: - environments, prerequisites, migrations, fixtures, roles and credentials needed, without secret values;
117: - known limitations, unresolved issues, preserved paths and relevant rollback dependencies;
118: - the meaning of each engineering evidence item, clearly labeled as a claim for independent QA.

If the manifest needs a factual amendment, the authorized owner records it; the Executor records independent identity in recon. 127: 
128: Execute the complete permitted readiness assessment in one run. Aggregate independent blockers instead of returning after each missing document when safe work remains possible. Stop immediately before a forbidden or unsafe action; do not continue through a compromised instrument or wrong target.

Main-dev, scratch and replica are module-specific approvals; never choose one by habit.

Specify expected results, independently derived references, negative controls, evidence, regression scope, ordering and dependencies. Identify what cannot yet be tested.

167: ## 11. Stops, findings and repairs
168: 
169: Use `STOP-*` identifiers for stops so they cannot be confused with phase names Q1/Q2/Q4/Q5. Each stop records phase, time, identity, affected checks, evidence, owner, required resolution and safe resume point.
170: 
171: | Stop | Trigger |
172: |---|---|
173: | STOP-IDENTITY | Wrong branch/candidate, unexpected product change or HEAD moved |
174: | STOP-CONTRACT | Grading depends on unresolved conflicting requirements or authority |
175: | STOP-ENV | Required target/data/tool/service unavailable or target mismatch |
176: | STOP-AUTH | Missing/invalid permitted credentials, unexpected identity, or auth outside authorization |
177: | STOP-AUTHORITY | Required action exceeds approved mutations, cost, data, Git or write-lane scope |
178: | STOP-INSTRUMENT | Measurement/helper control is untrustworthy; evidence cannot establish the claim |
179: | STOP-EVIDENCE | Evidence cannot be saved or exported with integrity |
180: | STOP-PRIVACY | Secret/protected-data exposure or unsafe retained artifact |
181: 
182: Do not use a stop as a reason to hide completed evidence. Supply a safe partial package when possible; withhold unsafe exports. Resume from preserved state after resolution rather than silently repeating the campaign.
183: 
184: Executor result labels are observations, not release decisions. Normalize PASS, FAIL, BLOCKED, NOT RUN and ADJUDICATE against the local governing vocabulary; where the governing documents use UNTESTED or PASS-PENDING-ADJUDICATION, preserve an explicit mapping. An unresolved discrepancy cannot become PASS through relabeling.
185: 
186: The QA Lead classifies acceptance failure, regression, environment/setup issue, instrument issue, contract gap, proven pre-existing defect, follow-up finding, enhancement or observation. Severity follows impact. Inherited conditions require evidence; neither charge them to a new module nor waive a release-blocking issue without the governing disposition.
187: 
188: For a genuine product defect: Lead disposition → Architect ruling/bounded repair instruction → Engineer repairs approved paths on the QA line → Director commits → Executor re-pins and performs Q4. Retest covers the repair diff, affected ACs and the full required regression suite. Carry forward only evidence whose applicability is demonstrated and whose original SHA/attempt remains visible. Live validation that the governing policy reserves for the final repaired candidate runs on that candidate. Preserve all prior attempts.
189: 
190: RRM-004 demonstrated no product repair round. This repair protocol carries forward the supplied governing doctrine; it is not a capability proved by that pilot.

192: ## 12. Browser work and credentials
193: 
194: The approved plan must define role × route/workflow × data/filter state × viewport × theme coverage and explain reductions. Do not hardcode Cyber Pharma's ADMIN/MEMBER or 375px into every app; use the actual contract. Check rendered behavior, effective theme, keyboard/focus, errors, loading/stale/rejection states and relevant access denials. Screenshots supplement assertions; neither screenshots alone nor green jsdom tests prove all browser behavior.
195: 
196: Reuse one in-memory authenticated context per role where safe. Separate roles and tenants. Record actual sign-in counts. Reusable regression tests are preferred when applicable; temporary module helpers are valid evidence tools. Promotion into maintained product tests is a separate authorized engineering change.
197: 
198: Credential automation is permitted only when the target, identities, runtime and tool policy allow it. The Director creates the declared ignored QA credential file, normally `.env.qa.local`, with module-specific keys. Do not reuse public `NEXT_PUBLIC_*` variables for QA passwords. Prove the file is ignored AND untracked, validate its schema without emitting values, and have only the authorized local helper read values into process memory. Keep credentials out of the app build/server environment, commands, logs, exceptions, screenshots, traces, JSON and ZIPs. A service-role/admin API key is not a substitute for proving a user's login and authorization.
199: 
200: If browser tooling requires human credential entry, use that supported flow and record the checkpoint. No automation plan overrides tool restrictions, MFA or access controls. Login/logout can create session/audit activity even when the business test is read-only. Backend, commerce and agent tests can mutate state or trigger costs; specify permitted effects and test targets before execution.
201: 
202: Privacy scanners must be validated with synthetic leak fixtures and clean controls. Scan selected export contents and expanded archive members; report safe counts/locations without echoing secrets. Raw authenticated traces can contain sensitive state even after login. Retain only approved sanitized evidence. Avoid persistent browser storage; if unavoidable, declare its temporary path and cleanup before use.

214: ## 14. Cleanup, certification and closeout
215: 
216: Two-shot QA preserves the distinction between product evidence, cleanup readiness and release. Routine safe hygiene—closing owned browsers, stopping owned QA servers, removing declared transient auth state—belongs in the phase's preauthorized exit steps. The final Q5 retention/cleanup decision remains governed by the QA Lead and the applicable cleanup policy: this section, `QA_PLAYBOOK.md` §14 (The Final Environment Reset Rule) and `RRM_PLAYBOOK.md` §10. It is a closure operation, not another full planning/testing campaign. This kit does not silently replace a required post-Gate-Q cleanup checkpoint.
217: 
218: At the approved cleanup point: scan values while the credential source exists; resolve/quarantine actual leaks without publishing them; delete the QA credential file and owned auth state under the declared authority; verify removal; pattern-scan retained outputs and the final export. Do not preserve credential values or hashes as evidence. After deletion, do not claim a new value scan happened; record the actual sequence and earlier source hashes. Active editor recovery files and unrelated processes are not automatically deleted. Director password rotation is separate and must be recorded as Director-reported unless independently evidenced.
219: 
220: The Lead defines the smallest durable evidence package sufficient for certification, reproduction and audit. Retain failed attempts and useful helpers when justified; remove disposable generated debris. Record precise paths and exclusions. Check current state before cleanup: already absent is verified absent, not newly deleted. Protected recovery/session files require their own authority.
221: 
222: The QA Lead issues the governing verdict: PASS, PASS WITH FOLLOW-UP FINDINGS, PASS WITH KNOWN RISK, FAIL or BLOCKED, with scope, candidate and limitations. No accepted requirement may silently become optional; meaningful risk acceptance follows Director authority. Product Gate Q, pilot-process evaluation, cleanup acceptance and Gate D remain separate claims.
223: 
224: Release to Architect closeout requires the accepted product verdict, required cleanup, durable records committed and the governed clean-tree condition. The Architect then directs bounded Engineer closeout; the Director commits, merges and pushes. Verify documentation-only successors before extending the recorded identity. Gate Q does not certify a deployed revision; Gate D is separately scoped and authorized when required.

227: 
228: Maintain `QAM_STATE.md` as a small index into authoritative records: phase, attempt, candidate, execution HEAD, contract/plan/approval identity, latest package/receipt, stop, next owner and next permitted action. Do not duplicate entire reports. Immutable exports retain their historical statements; later state entries supersede stale “pending” summaries with dated provenance.

This rule prevents both unsafe resumption and unnecessary restarts.
