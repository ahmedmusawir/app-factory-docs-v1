## Lessons Learned

- When a planning document cites IDs defined in a companion (CL-nn), grep the companion for the exact IDs before delivery; R0 cited CL-04…07 for items numbered CL-11…13 and §3.
- Authoring and ownership are different acts: an Engineer may draft an instrument of a QA rule, but only the owning seat (QA Lead) accepts it; label drafts so no reader mistakes authorship for approval.
- A current ruling settles what is drafted; it never backfills the provenance of an unsourced historical claim. Keep "governs drafting" and "authenticates the source" as separate statements.
- Write timestamps from the clock, not from an estimate of when a step will finish; two records had to be re-stamped and a ZIP rebuilt.
- A state machine in a template must have one vocabulary, written once, with every state owning a dispatch row and a next owner; a "waiting" state must say explicitly that nobody acts.
- Second time today: a timestamp written as "when this will be done" instead of "now" forced a ZIP rebuild. Write the clock value at the moment of writing, every time, or leave the field for the index to fill.
- "Accepted with exact amendments" is the Lead's decision on the reviewed version; the amended version carries that decision as its status, never a fresh approval. Label it with the decision, the date, and the pending step.
- A ruling can resolve a question in a way none of the drafted options anticipated (A.2: one continuing-branch pattern, not "two named patterns"); keep the R0 recommendation visible but marked superseded rather than rewriting history.
